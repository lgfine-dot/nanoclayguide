"Nanoclay" spans a price range of more than twenty-fold. The same word covers technical-grade sodium bentonite heading into a drilling mud at a few dollars per kilogram and a purified, precisely modified organoclay for a barrier film at well over a hundred — materials that share a mineral and almost nothing else commercially.

That spread is why *what does it cost?* has no clean answer, and why a quote is impossible to judge without knowing which of those products you are actually being sold.

**If you have a quote in front of you**, start with the [Nanoclay Pricing Guide](/blog/nanoclay-pricing-guide-2026/). It sets out the four things that genuinely drive price — purity and consistency, surface modification, particle-size control and exfoliation readiness, and volume — the rough tiers those produce, and what to ask for in a quote so you can tell whether you are paying for purity you do not need.

**If you are building a budget**, the quoted price is the wrong starting number. [The Total Cost of Nanoclay](/blog/total-cost-of-nanoclay-beyond-price-per-kilogram/) works through the layers underneath it: what you have to buy alongside the material, processing, yield and scrap, qualification and validation, and the cost of getting the grade wrong — which is routinely the largest line of all.

**If you are assessing supply risk**, [the global supply chain](/blog/global-nanoclay-supply-chain/) maps who mines, who refines, who modifies, and who sells, along with where concentration risk sits and which layer you should be buying from for your volume.

**If you are planning further out**, [the 2026–2035 market outlook](/blog/nanoclay-market-outlook-2026-2035/) covers what published forecasts agree on, why their headline numbers diverge so widely, and three scenarios to plan against rather than a single figure to trust.
