Choosing a nanoclay is mostly a process of elimination, and it goes wrong in two predictable ways: picking a mineral whose structure cannot do what you need, or picking the right mineral with the wrong surface modifier on it. This pillar covers both decisions.

**Start with the words.** [Bentonite, smectite, montmorillonite](/blog/bentonite-smectite-montmorillonite-terminology/) untangles a rock, a mineral group and a mineral that are routinely treated as synonyms. Until those are straight, no comparison between grades means very much.

**Then the survey.** [Nanoclay Types Compared](/blog/nanoclay-types-compared/) puts sodium montmorillonite, organically modified montmorillonite, halloysite nanotubes, kaolinite, sepiolite and palygorskite side by side, and ends with a decision framework rather than a table you are left to interpret alone.

**For the three you are most likely to be weighing**, [Montmorillonite vs. Kaolinite vs. Halloysite](/blog/montmorillonite-vs-kaolinite-vs-halloysite/) goes deeper on the structural differences that drive everything downstream, and sets out when each one is genuinely the right answer.

**If cost is the sticking point**, [Natural vs. Synthetic Nanoclays](/blog/natural-vs-synthetic-nanoclays-laponite-hectorite/) addresses the Laponite question directly: synthetic clays cost several times what mined bentonite does, they are worth it in a narrow set of applications, and the article frames the axis that actually decides it.

**And once the mineral is settled**, the modifier is the next decision, not an afterthought. [Choosing an Organoclay Modifier](/blog/choosing-organoclay-modifier-surfactant-chemistry/) covers the four variables you are really selecting between — and the thermal ceiling that suppliers rarely quote but your extruder will find.
