Nanoclay is a word used loosely enough that two people can agree they need some and mean genuinely different materials. This pillar exists to fix that before you spend money — the vocabulary, the structure, and the two numbers that predict most of what a clay will do.

**If you have five minutes**, read [What Is Nanoclay?](/blog/what-is-nanoclay/). It gives a practical definition, explains what makes a clay "nano" in the first place, covers the difference between a tactoid and an exfoliated platelet, and — usefully — spells out what nanoclay is *not*.

**If you have longer**, [What Are Nanoclays?](/blog/what-are-nanoclays-complete-guide/) is the fuller plain-language guide: how the layers are structured, the five nanoclays you will actually encounter, what they do in a formulation, and roughly what they cost.

**Then fix the vocabulary.** [Bentonite, smectite, montmorillonite](/blog/bentonite-smectite-montmorillonite-terminology/) sorts out three words that get used interchangeably and are not interchangeable at all. Getting this wrong is how specifications end up describing a rock when they meant a mineral.

**Two concepts do the real predictive work.** [Cation exchange capacity](/blog/cation-exchange-capacity-explained/) is the closest thing nanoclay has to a master variable — it is why montmorillonite swells and kaolinite does not, and why two clays sold under the same name can behave differently. [Exfoliation, intercalation and aspect ratio](/blog/exfoliation-intercalation-aspect-ratio-explained/) are the three terms behind every performance claim you will read, and the article is explicit about how to tell a real claim from a loose one.

Cover these five and the rest of this site reads much faster.
